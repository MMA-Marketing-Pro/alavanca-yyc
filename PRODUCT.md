# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three primary audiences shopping Calgary and Airdrie for combat sports training:

- **Adult prospects** — researching Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, Muay Thai, or MMA. Mix of total beginners (intimidated by fight gyms, want fitness + skill) and experienced practitioners shopping for a serious room. Comparing Alavanca against Infighting, Sublime BJJ, Team Fearless, and other Calgary academies.
- **Parents** — researching kids programs (Tiny Warriors 3–6, Kids BJJ, Kids Muay Thai) and summer camps. Care about safety, coach quality, location proximity, and whether the gym feels family-appropriate.
- **Women** — specifically researching the Shawnessy women's-only BJJ program. Often first-time martial artists who need the room to feel safe, supportive, and beginner-friendly before they'll book.

Secondary: existing members navigating schedules, locations, and coach pages.

The conversion target on every page is the same: **book a free trial class**.

## Product Purpose

Alavanca is a three-location Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, Muay Thai, and MMA academy serving Calgary (HQ + Shawnessy) and Airdrie. The website's job is to:

1. **Drive free-trial bookings** — every page funnels to `/free-class` → `/booking`. Conversion rate optimization is the #1 success metric.
2. **Establish location credibility** — three locations is a real differentiator in this market. The site should make the multi-location footprint feel like a strength, not a sprawl.
3. **Position Alavanca as the modern, premium choice** — not the cheapest gym, not the scariest gym, not a corporate chain. A clean, serious academy that takes the craft seriously and welcomes beginners.
4. **Showcase program range** without diluting focus — adult BJJ/Muay Thai/MMA leads, with kids programs and the women's-only BJJ program as supporting credibility.

Tagline: **"Decide to Rise."** This anchors hero moments and should feel earned, not slogan-y.

## Brand Personality

**Disciplined. Grounded. Welcoming. Modern.**

- **Disciplined** — the craft is real. Gracie lineage, structured curriculum, coaches who've been on the mat for decades. Copy should read confident and specific, never hyped.
- **Grounded** — no chest-thumping, no Instagram-flex energy. The testimonials carry the weight: members who've been training 10+ years, the 50-year-old who drives 2 hours each way. That tone runs through everything.
- **Welcoming** — the room is for beginners, kids, women, and lifers. Anti-intimidation is a core requirement. Imagery and copy should signal that walking in on day one is normal.
- **Modern** — clean visual language, premium materials, tight typography. The current Duda-template site reads dated and conservative; the redesign should feel like a 2026 sportswear brand, not a 2014 site builder.

Voice: confident, practical, no fluff. Direct response without being salesy. Earned authority — show, don't tell.

## Anti-references

Things this site must NOT look or feel like:

- **Generic Duda / Wix / GoDaddy site-builder templates** — the current site itself. Conservative navy/gray palette, weak hierarchy, cluttered nav, Mapbox blocks where hero photography belongs.
- **Blood-and-cage UFC fight-poster brutalism** — black-red-skull-flames energy. Wrong audience, scares off parents and beginner women.
- **Corporate gym chains** — LA Fitness, GoodLife, Anytime Fitness. Stock-photo group fitness, blue-and-yellow chain branding, "membership special" banner energy.
- **Pro-fighters-only signaling** — visuals or copy implying you need to already be tough to walk in. Cuts directly against the welcoming requirement.
- **Grungy / dirty / dive-gym aesthetics** — distressed textures, ransom-note typography, basement-fight-club vibe. Alavanca is a clean, professional, premium room, and the site needs to reflect that.

## Design Principles

1. **Premium over pumped-up.** Modern sportswear-brand restraint, not combat-sports loudness. Tight typography, intentional whitespace, real photography. The brand should read closer to Nike / Tracksmith / Equinox than to Tapout / UFC Gym.

2. **Beginner-safe by default.** Every page should feel inviting to a first-time student — parent, woman, complete novice. No imagery, copy, or visual cue that implies "you have to be tough already." This rules out a whole register of combat-sports clichés.

3. **Locations as a strength, not a sprawl.** Three locations is a competitive moat in this market. Treat the multi-location footprint as an architectural feature: every program page should clearly map to which locations offer it, and location pages should feel like distinct rooms, not duplicates.

4. **Conversion is structural, not stuck on.** "Try a free class" is the only meaningful action. Don't bury it under three CTA tiers; let it live in the navigation, the hero, the section breaks, and the footer — without ever feeling like a hard sell. Every page is a funnel page.

5. **Earned authority over claimed authority.** The 10-year-tenured testimonials, the Gracie lineage mention, the long-running coaches — these are the credibility carriers. Lead with members and coaches; lead away from generic stock fitness imagery and adjective-stuffed marketing prose.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** for body text contrast (4.5:1 minimum). The current site fails this on the existing accent (`#587291` on `#EFEFEF`) — the redesign must not repeat that mistake.
- Reduced-motion users get static or near-static treatments — the site uses scroll-driven reveals and a kinetic marquee that must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Imagery should reflect the actual community: mixed ages, mixed genders, kids and adults, beginners and veterans. The women's program and kids programs especially need authentic representation, not stock combat photos.
- Mobile-first. Most prospects are searching on phones — every conversion path must work on a 375px screen before it works on desktop.
